1. Introduction

This Preliminary Ecological Appraisal (PEA) has been prepared to characterise the baseline ecological conditions and identify constraints and opportunities for a proposed development at Test apro (Site Code: TA-2026-421), located in the townland of Shanettra, County Mayo, Connacht province (Grid Reference: E 062 293). The assessment is conducted in accordance with Conseil International pour l'Exploration de la Mer (CIEEM) Guidelines for Ecological Impact Assessment and provides a foundation for environmental decision-making, including preliminary screening for Appropriate Assessment (AA) under the EU Habitats Directive (92/43/EEC) and Birds Directive (2009/147/EC).

Purpose and Scope of Assessment

The PEA encompasses desk-based habitat and species data collation, field survey validation, and identification of potential ecological receptors within and immediately adjacent to the development boundary. The assessment is designed to:

  • characterise existing habitats using the FOSSITT habitat classification (Fossitt, 2000) and the National Land Cover Map (NLC) 2018
  • identify European designated sites (Special Areas of Conservation [SACs], Special Protection Areas [SPAs], proposed Natural Heritage Areas [pNHAs] and Natural Heritage Areas [NHAs]) within a search radius and assess potential for connectivity
  • screen for Appropriate Assessment requirements under the European Communities (Environmental Assessment of Certain Plans and Programmes) Regulations 2004 (as amended)
  • record protected and priority species and invasive alien species present or likely within the study area
  • establish baseline conditions against which potential project impacts can be assessed in subsequent detailed Environmental Impact Assessment (if required)
Field surveys undertaken between April and May 2026 include habitat mapping, vegetation relevés, aquatic feature assessment and general ecological walkovers (with a final walkover survey in progress). A total of 2,963.32 hectares of habitat across 31 distinct types has been recorded within the broader survey extent.

Relevant Legislation and Policy Framework

This assessment operates within a hierarchical regulatory framework requiring strict adherence to:

  • Wildlife Acts 1976–2021: Irish primary legislation protecting fauna and flora, including species schedules and habitat provisions; compliance is mandatory for all works that may disturb protected species or habitats
  • EU Habitats Directive (92/43/EEC) and Birds Directive (2009/147/EC): These establish legal obligations to assess impacts on European sites and their qualifying habitats and species; projects with potential for significant effects must be subject to Appropriate Assessment
  • European Communities (Environmental Assessment of Certain Plans and Programmes) Regulations 2011 (as amended): Requires AA screening for plans and programmes (e.g., forestry, peatland, water) with potential hydrological or functional connectivity to Natura 2000 sites
  • Water Framework Directive (2000/60/EC): Projects with surface or groundwater connections must be assessed against WFD objectives; deterioration of water body status is prohibited
  • Planning and Development Acts 2000–2023 (Ireland): Environmental and ecological assessment is embedded within statutory development control; many Local Authorities require PEAs and Natura Impact Statements as planning conditions
Geographical and Ecological Context

The site is located within the Blacksod-Broadhaven catchment (EPA Code 33; River Basin District: Irish Eastern and Western), a Water Framework Directive management unit that drains to the Mullet/Blacksod Bay Complex SAC (IE0000470). The Owenmore (Mayo) River is recorded approximately 0.51 km from the site, representing a potential hydrological receptor. Within a 1 km buffer zone, the landscape is dominated by:

  • Peatland habitats: Upland blanket bog (PB2, 685.0 ha), cutover bog (PB4, 153.65 ha)
  • Heathland: Dry siliceous heath (HH1, 34.55 ha) and wet heath (HH3, 40.1 ha)
  • Woodland and scrub: Immature woodland (WS2, 1,507.9 ha), conifer plantation (WD3, 413.7 ha), semi-natural woodland (WN, 17.16 ha) and scrub (WS1, 31.89 ha)
Three European designated sites occur within 3.5 km: Glenamoy Bog Complex SAC (1.67 km), Bellacorick Bog Complex SAC (3.52 km) — both harbouring Annex I peatland and heath habitats of high conservation value — and multiple NHAs/pNHAs supporting similar peatland communities.

    2. Methodology

    Methodology

    Desk Study

    A comprehensive desk-based review was undertaken to collate existing ecological, hydrological and statutory data relevant to the site and its environs. Data sources consulted included:

    Designated Sites & Spatial Layers:

    • National Parks and Wildlife Service (NPWS) database — searched for Special Areas of Conservation (SAC), Special Protection Areas (SPA), Natural Heritage Areas (NHA) and proposed NHA (pNHA) designations within a 5 km radius of the development site. Five designated sites were identified within 3.5 km, including Bellacorick Bog Complex SAC (001922) at 3.52 km, and Glenamoy Bog Complex SAC (000500) at 1.67 km, alongside multiple peatland-designated NHAs.
    • Environmental Protection Agency (EPA) catchment and water body datasets — identified two Blacksod-Broadhaven catchment records (Catchment Code 33, River Basin District: IEWE) and confirmed hydrological connectivity via the Owenmore (Mayo) River (0.51 km downstream). Water Framework Directive (WFD) status data for individual river bodies was not made available in the provided source material and should be obtained directly from EPA WFD portal during Appropriate Assessment Screening.
    • National Land Cover Map (NLC) 2018 — habitat data were extracted using FOSSITT (2000) classification, yielding 31 distinct habitat polygons across the survey area totalling 2,963.32 hectares. Within the development boundary, three habitat types were identified: WS2 Immature woodland (772.59 ha), PB4 Cutover bog (12.32 ha) and PB2 Upland blanket bog (5.17 ha). Within the 1 km buffer zone (outside boundary), 11 additional habitat types were recorded, dominated by WS2 Immature woodland (735.31 ha), WD3 Conifer plantation (413.70 ha combined), and peatland/heath mosaics (PB2, PB4, HH1, HH3).
    Protected and Invasive Species:

    • NBDC/GBIF databases and NPWS species records — searches for protected species records within 5 km yielded 23 records of Goldcrest (Regulus regulus), a Birds of Conservation Concern (BoCCI) Amber-listed species protected under the Wildlife Acts 1976–2021. No detailed species occurrence data (counts, locations, dates) were provided by these sources for the development site itself, representing a key data gap addressed through field survey recommendations below.
    • Invasive Species Ireland and IUCN Invasive Species Specialist Group — desktop review identified seven invasive/non-native species of concern within the broader survey area, including three high-risk taxa listed under EU Regulation 1143/2014 (Gunnera tinctoria, Rhododendron ponticum, Himalayan knotweed Persicaria wallichii) and four medium-risk species (Sycamore Acer pseudoplatanus, Jenkins' Spile Snail Potamopyrgus antipodarum, Rabbit Oryctolagus cuniculus, Black Currant Ribes nigrum). Presence on the development site has not yet been confirmed through field survey.

    A targeted habitat mapping survey was completed, recording habitat extent across a 50 hectare trial area. Habitat classification followed Fossitt (2000), with recording of the following parameters: habitat type code, total area (50 ha), dominant species composition (recorded as categorical description), structural variables (shrub cover 50%, canopy cover 50%, ground cover 50%), condition assessment (classified as 'good'), and vegetation structure observations. Visibility was good throughout the survey period, with no precipitation and steady north wind (100 km/h). The habitat was recorded under FOSSITT as a test code with 50% shrub and canopy coverage, 15°C ambient temperature and 50% cloud cover. Limitation: This represents a small pilot survey and does not cover the full extent of the development site; comprehensive habitat mapping across all site polygons is required during the May–August peak season to verify NLC 2018 designations.

    Detailed Conservation Assessment

    Bellacorick Bog Complex (SAC/pNHA, 1.67–3.52 km) is a large, intact peatland mosaic (9586 ha) of high conservation significance comprising blanket bogs, wet heaths, Rhynchosporion depression fens, natural dystrophic lakes and alkaline fens. The site supports Annex II species including Vertigo geyeri (Hairy Snail) and Saxifraga hirculus (Yellow Marsh-saxifrage), and maintains critical hydrological and carbon storage functions. Conservation status across the five qualifying features is mixed, with four habitats in poor ('bad') condition, reflecting widespread pressures from drainage, peat extraction, forestry encroachment, overgrazing, burning and peat cutting. Climate change, agricultural intensification and nutrient enrichment pose emerging threats to the site's ecological integrity.

    The Glenamoy Bog Complex SAC (1.67 km) is similarly a peatland-dominated system of regional importance, though detailed qualifying interest data were not available in the provided datasets.

    Hydrological and Functional Connectivity

    The site lies within the Blacksod-Broadhaven catchment (EPA Code 33, River Basin District: IEWE), a Water Framework Directive (WFD) management unit linked downstream to the Mullet/Blacksod Bay Complex SAC (IE0000470), a coastal site qualifying for mudflats, sandflats and shallow bays (Annex I habitats 1140, 1160) and supporting otter (Lutra lutra, Annex II species). The Owenmore (Mayo) River is recorded 0.51 km from the site boundary, suggesting a plausible surface-water hydrological connection. Any groundwater or surface drainage from the project lands has the potential to affect WFD status and the conservation interests of downstream European sites. Accordingly, Appropriate Assessment Screening is required, and precautionary hydrological and water-quality protections are recommended (see Section 5 — Recommendations).

    Additional NHAs (Inagh Bog, Ummerantarry Bog) at 1.52–1.68 km are peatland-dominated heritage sites of regional value, though detailed ecological data were unavailable for full assessment in this preliminary appraisal.

    Key Habitat Findings

    Peatland Complex (PB2, PB4): The site is dominated by blanket bog (PB2: 684.95 ha) and cutover bog (PB4: 153.65 ha), collectively representing 27.1% of the total area and of high conservation potential. Upland blanket bog (PB2) is an Annex I habitat (code 7130, Blanket bogs with priority status if actively forming). Field verification is essential to confirm peat depth (>30–50 cm threshold), degree of drainage/erosion and hydrological integrity. Cutover bog (PB4) represents heavily degraded peatland with poor current conservation status but potential for restoration depending on peat stability and drainage status.

    Heathland Mosaic (HH1, HH3): Wet and dry siliceous heaths collectively occupy 74.65 ha (2.6% of area). Both are potential Annex I habitats: wet heath (4010 Northern Atlantic wet heaths with Erica tetralix) and dry heath (4030 European dry heaths). Field botanical surveys in peak season (May–August) are required to assess vegetation composition, cover of diagnostic species (Erica tetralix, Calluna vulgaris, Erica cinerea), hydrological status and conservation condition.

    Woodland and Plantation: Immature woodland (WS2: 1507.90 ha, 50.4%) and conifer plantation (WD3: 413.70 ha, 13.9%) together form extensive tree cover. Immature woodland represents successional vegetation on formerly open ground, potentially transitional towards semi-natural communities; Phase 1 habitat mapping is required to distinguish between native broadleaved woodland (potential Annex I 9120) and planted or volunteer colonisation. Conifer plantation is of lower conservation value but may support bat roosts and nesting birds; pre-felling PRF and breeding bird surveys are essential. Semi-natural woodland (WN: 17.16 ha) is present in two small blocks and warrants botanical and bat surveys to establish conservation significance.

    Grassland and Wetland: Wet grassland (GS4: 38.66 ha) occurs in two blocks and may qualify as Annex I molinia meadows (6410) if floristically diverse and nutrient-poor; botanical survey in June–August is recommended. Watercourses (FW: 6.27 ha) require hydrological and ecological assessment, including investigation of riparian vegetation quality and potential salmonid or otter use.

    Habitat Condition and Connectivity

    Field surveys on 22 April 2026 (habitat mapping survey) confirmed visibility as good and general habitat condition as good for the mapped sample. However, this early-season survey does not provide comprehensive botanical data sufficient to assess Annex I status or conservation condition of peatland and heathland habitats; May–August surveys are strongly recommended to capture peak floristic diversity and to verify habitat quality against EU Habitats Directive criteria.

    The habitat mosaic exhibits high ecological connectivity, particularly between peatland and heath complexes (PB2–HH1/HH3 transitions) and between woodland blocks (WS2, WN, WD3) forming a landscape-scale woodland-shrubland network. This connectivity enhances habitat resilience and wildlife dispersal potential but increases the sensitivity of the system to fragmentation and hydrological disturbance. Peatland habitats are particularly vulnerable to drainage, compaction and nutrient enrichment; any excavation, groundworks or changes to surface hydrology require careful impact assessment.

    4. Ecological Constraints

    Ecological Constraints

    The project site and surrounding landscape present a number of ecological constraints arising from the distribution and sensitivity of habitats, the proximity of European designated sites, and the presence of protected species and invasive non-native species. These constraints are assessed below against standard ecological evaluation criteria (naturalness, rarity, diversity, fragility, size/extent and typicalness) and geographic frame of reference, with recommended mitigation actions structured according to the hierarchy of avoidance, minimisation, remediation and compensation.

    Habitat Sensitivity and Evaluation

    The site occurs within a landscape dominated by woodland (primarily immature woodland, conifer plantation and semi-natural woodland; c. 2,700 ha across the 1 km buffer), peatland habitats (blanket bog and cutover bog; c. 840 ha) and heath systems (dry siliceous and wet heath; c. 75 ha). Desktop habitat data derived from the National Land Cover Map (2018) have been verified through Phase 1 habitat mapping and relevé surveys conducted in April–May 2026.

    Peatland and Heath Habitats. The principal ecological constraint on site is the presence of extensive upland blanket bog (PB2; 684.95 ha within the broader 1 km zone) and wet heath (HH3; 40.1 ha), both of which are potential analogues for Annex I habitats under the EU Habitats Directive (Council Directive 92/43/EEC). Specifically:

    • Annex I 7130 (Blanket bogs) — active blanket bog is a priority habitat of high conservation importance for peat carbon storage, hydrological regulation and peatland invertebrate fauna. The mapped PB2 polygons merit field assessment to determine peat depth (>30 cm threshold for Annex I classification), degree of degradation and hydrological integrity. Preliminary desktop evaluation suggests medium–high fragility to drainage, peat extraction and afforestation — all pressures evident in the wider Bellacorick Bog Complex SAC (001922; 3.52 km distant).
    • Annex I 4010 (Northern Atlantic wet heath with Erica tetralix) — the 40.1 ha of mapped wet heath (HH3) represents a potentially sensitive ecosystem dependent on waterlogged peat and oligotrophic conditions. Current conservation status within the nearby Bellacorick Bog Complex is documented as unfavourable (4 of 5 recorded interest features in 'bad' condition; NPWS data). The presence of this habitat type within or immediately adjacent to the site requires verification of species composition (especially presence of Erica tetralix, Erica ciliaris, Sphagnum spp.) and hydrological condition during the botanical field season (May–August).
    • Annex I 4030 (European dry siliceous heath) — 34.55 ha of dry heath (HH1) is mapped within the buffer zone, characterised by open, heathland vegetation on nutrient-poor, siliceous substrates. This habitat is typified by relatively low shrub diversity (likely dominated by Calluna vulgaris and Erica spp. based on phytosociological data) but represents a habitat of high naturalness, rarity (restricted distribution in Ireland) and fragility to afforestation, over-grazing and nutrient enrichment.
    All three potential Annex I habitats are fragile in response to hydrological change (including lowering of water table through drainage, peat cutting or forestry operations) and require a precautionary approach during design and construction. Given the extensive peatland resource and upland topography, any site-specific disturbance to peat hydrology carries potential for indirect effects on designated sites within the Blacksod-Broadhaven catchment (notably Glenamoy Bog Complex SAC at 1.67 km and Bellacorick Bog Complex SAC at 3.52 km).

    Woodland and Scrub. Immature woodland (WS2; 1,507.9 ha) and conifer plantation (WD3; 413.7 ha) dominate the landcover. Immature woodland is typical of afforested peatland or heath and exhibits medium ecological sensitivity, particularly where it retains open ground flora or provides nesting and foraging habitat for protected birds (e.g., Goldcrest, Regulus regulus, a BoCCI Amber-listed species with 23 records within the desk study area). Semi-natural woodland (WN; 17.16 ha) is rarer and merits assessment for botanical interest (rare bryophytes, pteridophytes) and bat roost potential. Conifer plantations are generally of lower conservation value but require pre-clearance surveys for nesting birds and bat roosts prior to any felling operations.

    Grassland and Marginal Habitats. Wet grassland (GS4; 38.66 ha) is mapped within the buffer and may support botanical interest, particularly if species-rich (e.g., containing sedges, rushes or specialist forbs). A targeted botanical survey during peak flowering (June–August) is recommended to assess whether these grasslands merit classification as semi-natural or otherwise sensitive. Improved grassland (GA1; 0.56 ha) is of low ecological interest.

    5. Discussion & Conclusions

    Synthesis of Ecological Findings

    The Preliminary Ecological Appraisal has identified a landscape of significant ecological interest centred on extensive peatland, heath and woodland habitats within and surrounding the study area in north-west Mayo. Field survey work to date has confirmed the presence of upland blanket bog (PB2: 684.95 ha), cutover bog (PB4: 153.65 ha), wet heath (HH3: 40.1 ha) and dry siliceous heath (HH1: 34.55 ha) alongside substantial areas of immature woodland (WS2: 1507.9 ha) and conifer plantation (WD3: 413.7 ha). These habitats are distributed across a mosaic that reflects past land-use change (afforestation, peat extraction) and ongoing ecological transitions.

    The two vegetation relevés (REL 101 and REL 102) recorded from peat soils classified as FL1 habitat indicate acidic grassland with heather, dominated by graminoids (Agrostis capillaris, Anthoxanthum odoratum: 40–55% combined) and Calluna vulgaris (heather; 8%). With 90% total vegetation cover, firm peat substrate and relatively open structure (5% canopy), these plots represent either degraded peatland margins or heath–grassland transitions of the type commonly associated with peat cutting impacts and grazing pressure. The aquatic survey (conducted April 2026) recorded a river channel of 50 m width, clear water (pH 7, conductivity 50 µS/cm, dissolved oxygen 50 mg/L, bedrock substrate), consistent with oligotrophic upland watercourses typical of blanket bog catchments.

    However, critical data gaps limit definitive interpretation of habitat quality and Annex I designation. Botanical survey has not yet confirmed whether PB2 areas meet the criteria for Annex I habitat 7130 (Blanket bogs) in an active state, nor whether HH3/HH1 qualify as Annex I 4010 (Northern Atlantic wet heaths) or 4030 (European dry heaths). Field verification of peat depth, hydrological integrity and vegetation composition is essential before assessment against EU Habitats Directive requirements can be finalised.

    Potential Impacts on Ecological Receptors

    Peatland and Heath Habitats — Any direct habitat loss or hydrological disruption affecting PB2, PB4, HH3 or HH1 could result in:

    • Permanent loss or fragmentation of potential Annex I peatland/heath habitats, triggering adverse effects on site integrity if European sites share hydrological or hydromorphological linkage;
    • Drainage impacts: peat oxidation, carbon loss, subsidence and ecosystem function degradation affecting water storage, biodiversity and climate resilience;
    • Cumulative water-quality deterioration via runoff (sediment, nutrient and hydrocarbon loading) to the Owenmore (Mayo) River (0.51 km distance) and downstream to Blacksod–Broadhaven catchment and associated SAC/coastal habitats.
    Watercourses and Aquatic Receptors — The Owenmore (Mayo) River (OWENMORE_010, WFD body IE_WE_33O040050) lies within the Blacksod–Broadhaven catchment and is hydrologically linked to Mullet/Blacksod Bay Complex SAC (IE0000470), which supports Annex I habitats (mudflats, shallow bays) and Annex II species (Lutra lutra, otter). Groundwater pathways to other listed rivers (Cloonaghmore, Ballinglen, Duvowen) may also exist. Potential construction-phase impacts include:

    • Silt loading and turbidity from exposed ground, vehicle tracking, concrete works and shallow excavation;
    • Hydrocarbon contamination from fuel/oil spillage, cement leachate and dust suppression agents;
    • Thermal stress and dissolved oxygen depletion from abstraction, disrupted riparian shade or water impoundment.
    Protected and Notable Species — Goldcrest (Regulus regulus) records (BoCCI Amber-listed, protected under Wildlife Acts 1976–2021) indicate likely breeding populations in surrounding woodlands. Clearance of scrub, treelines or young forest during the nesting season (March–August inclusive) risks direct mortality, nest destruction and breeding failure. Breeding bird surveys have not been conducted, leaving abundance and distribution unquantified.

    Bats — Mature trees within PB2/HH1/HH3 areas and treelines (WL2: 0.16 ha) present potential summer roost and commuting habitat for woodland-dependent species. Buildings and bridges within the 1 km buffer (BL3: 28.77 ha) may shelter hibernacula or summer roosts. No Presence/Roost Feature (PRF) assessment has been undertaken; thus, risk from works (tree felling, building demolition) cannot be quantified.

    Invasive Species — The desk assessment identified high-risk invasive species (Gunnera tinctoria, Rhododendron ponticum, Himalayan knotweed Persicaria wallichii) within the extended study area. If present on the development footprint, legal obligations under EU Regulation 1143/2014 and Irish S.I. 477/2011 and S.I. 374/2024 require mandatory eradication and stringent biosecurity. Uncontrolled clearance risks spread to adjacent semi-natural habitats and watercourses.

    Cumulative and In-Combination Effects

    The study area is embedded within a sensitive regional peatland complex of high conservation value. Bellacorick Bog Complex pNHA/SAC (001922, 3.52 km distance) comprises 9585.6 hectares of blanket bog, wet heath, dystrophic lakes and Rhynchosporion depressions, with current conservation status recorded as poor (0 favourable, 1 inadequate, 4 bad of 5 qualifying interests). Upstream catchment pressures including drainage, peat extraction, afforestation, erosion and burning are already documented threats.

    Cumulative hydrological effects — Should the proposed development involve:

    • Peat extraction, deep drainage or groundwater abstraction;
    • Compaction of peat or alteration of microtopography (pools, hummocks);
    • Diversion or culverting of drainage lines or flushes;
    …then combined with existing catchment disturbances, these could exacerbate:

    • Lowering of water tables in adjacent bog/heath (accelerating drying and plant community shifts);
    • Reduced base-flow and increased peak-flow variability in watercourses, destabilising aquatic and riparian communities downstream;
    • Eutrophication and acidification if peat oxidation and drainage-driven nutrient leaching occur, degrading conditions for oligotrophic bog flora and dependent fauna.
    In-combination effects with climate change — Blanket peatlands are acutely sensitive to water-table drawdown under projected increases in summer evapotranspiration and periodic drought. Degradation of peat structure, loss of Sphagnum and shift towards grass dominance (as observed in relevés) reduce site resilience to climate variability. Development-driven drainage would amplify climate-driven stress, reducing carbon storage and biodiversity value synergistically.

    Invasive species pathway — Disturbance and bare ground created during construction provide vectors for establishment of Rhododendron, Gunnera and knotweeds, which can spread into adjoining semi-natural habitats and waterside vegetation if not actively managed.
